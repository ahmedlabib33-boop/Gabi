Decision logic

Find the buyer and specification first, then source matching waste, calculate all transaction costs, and purchase only when the remaining contribution and cash cycle are acceptable.

Expansion logic

Broker → selective aggregation → small sorting operation → baling → fiber opening/cleaning → industrial processing, with an investment gate between every stage.

Core rule: do not buy “one ton of textile waste.” Buy a known material grade with a known buyer, a known yield expectation, and a known maximum buying price.

Field validation required

Supply side

Priority feedstock is pre-consumer industrial textile waste: cutting waste, yarn waste, rejected rolls, offcuts and leftovers from garment factories, textile mills, knitting facilities, workshops and traders.

Demand side

Potential outlets include recycled-fiber processors, spinning mills, nonwoven manufacturers, filling and insulation producers, industrial wiping businesses, padding producers, traders and exporters.

Important: sector size does not prove your deal economics. Buyer specification, actual price, monthly capacity and payment terms must be verified.

Location formula

Yield

Saleable kg ÷ purchased kg. This must be measured once physical sorting starts.

Traceability

Every load should carry supplier, date, grade, gross weight, reject weight, saleable weight and buyer record.

Weight control

Use verified scale records and compare supplier vs buyer weighbridge results to detect systematic loss or fraud.

Do not buy initially

Truck • forklift • industrial baler • shredder • fiber opener • large conveyor • industrial property.

Possible micro-start tools

Verified platform scale • bags • labels • pallets • hand trolley • PPE • extinguishers • basic inventory and transaction records.

Warehouse gate

Rent only when storage or sorting limitations are demonstrably blocking profitable trades, and the additional contribution covers rent, labor, safety and inventory cost.

Machine gate

Buy a machine only when its measurable transport savings, processing premium or throughput gain exceed depreciation, finance, energy, labor, maintenance and space cost.

Licensing, HSE & Compliance

Brokerage, collection, storage, sorting and treatment may not share the same regulatory requirements.

Verify before commitment

Collection & transport

Where the activity legally falls under non-hazardous waste collection/transport, applicable Waste Management Regulatory Authority requirements must be confirmed before operation.

Treatment / processing

Shredding, opening, cleaning and other treatment can move the business into a different regulatory category from pure trading or brokerage.

Site & HSE

Dry textile stock creates combustible load. The operating site must address fire protection, housekeeping, ventilation, emergency access, electrical safety and storage zoning.
