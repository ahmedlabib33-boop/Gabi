Startup strategy

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Founder capital ceiling

< EGP 1m

The objective is not to spend the available ceiling, but to launch with the minimum possible cash exposure.

Initial fixed assets

Minimal

No truck, industrial recycling line or large warehouse should be purchased at the beginning.

Initial business model

Hybrid

Broker low-certainty loads and physically aggregate only materials where handling creates additional margin.

Investment principle

Proof first

Validate supply, buyers, prices, yield and cash cycle before investing in machinery.

Core rule: Find the buyer, required specification and selling price before paying the supplier.
Low-capital entry → market validation → aggregation → processing → industrial scale. 02
Market context

EGYPTIAN MARKET OPPORTUNITY

Supply-side logic

Egypt has a significant textile, garment, spinning and fabric-processing base. These industries continuously generate cutting waste, yarn waste, rejected fabric, offcuts and other pre-consumer textile residues.

Priority supplier categories include garment factories, textile mills, knitting operations, workshops, cutting facilities and fabric traders.

Demand-side logic

Potential buyers include recycled-fiber processors, spinning mills, nonwoven producers, filling manufacturers, insulation manufacturers, industrial wiping companies, padding producers, traders and exporters.

Market size alone does not prove feasibility. Real supplier volumes and actual buyer prices must be verified transaction by transaction.

Priority geographical clusters to investigate

10th of Ramadan Obour Badr Cairo Mahalla Sadat Ismailia Alexandria Borg El Arab
Location rule: Select the operating corridor based on available tons × contribution margin minus transport cost, not simply on the cheapest warehouse rent.
Supplier density, buyer access and logistics determine the best starting corridor. 03
Material intelligence

WHAT EXACTLY ARE YOU BUYING?

Material Classification Quality check Commercial approach
White cotton cutting waste COT-WHT Fiber purity, elastane %, moisture, contamination Separate carefully; potentially high sorting value.
Colored cotton waste COT-COL Color, fiber purity, dust, foreign materials Separate by color groups when buyer premium justifies it.
Polyester waste PES Composition, color, coatings, contamination Trade against buyer-specific specifications.
Cotton/polyester blend PC-BLEND Approximate fiber ratio and cleanliness Confirm buyer acceptance before purchase.
Denim waste DEN Cotton %, elastane, color and contamination Target specialized recyclers/buyers.
Mixed / unsorted waste MIX Sortability, foreign material, moisture and reject % Avoid speculative purchase until recovery yield is known.
Rejected rolls / remnants ROLL Dimensions, usability, defects and composition Evaluate reuse/resale before recycling.
Never buy “one ton of textile waste” as one undifferentiated material. Buy a known material specification with an identified exit market.
Material knowledge protects both margin and working capital. 04
Startup architecture

THREE STARTUP MODELS

Lowest capital

A — Pure Broker

Supplier → Buyer

Arrange the commercial transaction and logistics while avoiding inventory whenever possible.

Strength: very low fixed-cost exposure.

Weakness: lower control and risk of being bypassed.

Higher value capture

B — Micro Aggregation

Supplier → Small sorting point → Buyer

Collect smaller lots, sort, grade and consolidate them into commercially attractive quantities.

Strength: potentially higher contribution/kg.

Weakness: rent, labor, fire risk and working-capital exposure.

Recommended base case

C — Hybrid

Broker + selective physical aggregation

Broker unfamiliar, low-margin or uncertain material; physically aggregate repeat grades with known buyers and measurable sorting premiums.

Strength: balances cash protection with margin discovery.

Recommended startup base: Hybrid operation, progressing into physical aggregation only after recurring profitable transactions are established.
Use the market to decide where physical handling adds value. 05

→

→
4 — Decision Buy only when the remaining contribution and cash cycle satisfy your minimum target.

Maximum purchase price formula

Buyer price − logistics − handling − sorting − packaging − expected loss − overhead − risk reserve − target profit

Selling price creates the ceiling for the purchase price. 06

900 kg

KPI Formula
Saleable yield Saleable kg ÷ purchased kg
Purchase cost per saleable kg Total material purchase cost ÷ saleable kg
Net contribution Revenue − material − logistics − handling − sorting − transaction costs

Working capital

CASH BEFORE MACHINES

Pay supplier

Often at pickup or after weighing.

Hold inventory

Each inventory day consumes cash and increases fire and price risk.

Deliver buyer

Buyer confirms final weight and quality.

Collect cash

Target immediate or short payment terms in the early stage.

Founder cash policy

Keep most available capital liquid until repeat transactions are proven. The EGP 1 million ceiling is not money that must be spent.

Early controls

Define a maximum inventory value, maximum inventory days, maximum exposure to one buyer and maximum supplier advance.

Preferred early transaction: buyer confirmed → supplier pickup → same-day or next-day delivery → rapid payment.
Working capital is likely to be the first meaningful financing requirement. 12
Regulatory pathway

EGYPT LICENSING & COMPLIANCE

Collection & transport of non-hazardous waste

Where the activity legally falls within regulated non-hazardous waste collection and transportation, the applicable Waste Management Regulatory Authority requirements should be confirmed before operations.

WMRA: official collection/transport licensing information

Waste treatment facility

Mechanical processing, shredding, opening, cleaning or other treatment may move the business into a different regulatory category from simple brokerage or trading. Confirm the exact activity classification before acquiring a processing location or equipment.

WMRA: official waste-treatment information

Important: Do not assume brokerage, trading, collection, transport, storage, sorting and treatment have identical licensing requirements. Confirm the precise legal classification with the competent Egyptian authorities before commitment.
Regulatory verification must precede the warehouse and machinery commitment. 13

Emergency exits Essential
Electrical safety Essential
Fire protection & housekeeping Essential

Fire is a core risk

Dry textile inventory can create a high combustible load. Dust, poor housekeeping and ignition sources can increase the hazard substantially.

Lease gate

Rent a site only when lack of storage or sorting capability is demonstrably preventing profitable transactions or when the additional contribution covers all new fixed costs.

Safety requirements increase with inventory volume and processing intensity. 14
Future processing

MACHINERY INVESTMENT GATES

Gate 1 — Scale, bags and handling tools

Purchase basic equipment only where it improves weight control, traceability, safety or transaction accuracy.

Gate 2 — Baler

Purchase only when transport savings, improved selling price and additional throughput exceed depreciation, finance, electricity, labor, maintenance and space cost.

Gate 3 — Cutter / shredder / fiber opener

Invest only after the measured processing premium exceeds the full conversion cost with sufficient downside protection.

Gate 4 — Industrial recycling line

Compare 250 kg/h, 500 kg/h and 1,000 kg/h alternatives only after verified supply and buyer demand can support them.

Electricity

Processing-machine economics must use the actual electricity tariff applicable to the selected Egyptian site, voltage level and customer category at the date of investment.

EgyptERA: published electricity tariff information

Machinery should be justified by measured economics, not by appearance. 15
Funding strategy

HOW TO BECOME FUNDABLE

Weak funding story

“I have a textile recycling idea and need money to buy machines.”

Strong funding story

“We have traded documented quantities from repeat suppliers to repeat buyers, achieved a measured contribution per kg, controlled inventory days and now require equipment that will demonstrably improve capacity or margin.”

Evidence lenders/investors need

Invoices • weighbridge tickets • bank statements • supplier records • buyer history • inventory records • payment history • licenses • tax records • monthly management accounts • margin history.

Funding sequence

Founder cash → customer/supplier terms → documented turnover → working-capital finance → equipment finance → strategic investor for larger industrial expansion.

Egyptian MSME support and business-development channels can be investigated after the legal structure, business case and initial commercial evidence are prepared.
Central Bank of Egypt: Business Development Services / NilePreneurs information
Finance becomes easier when the project has measurable evidence. 16
Risk management

RISK REGISTER

Risk Failure mode Control
Supply Factory stops supplying or available quantity drops. Use multiple suppliers and track tons/month by source.
Buyer Buyer reduces price or stops purchasing. Maintain several approved buyers for each important grade.
Quality Material does not match specification. Inspect, sample, photograph and classify before purchase.
Weight Supplier and buyer weights differ. Use verified scales and documented weighing tickets.
Price Price falls while material is in inventory. Short inventory cycle and confirmed exit buyer.
Credit Buyer delays or fails to pay. Use cash/short terms initially and establish exposure limits.
Fire Stored dry textile waste ignites. Housekeeping, storage controls, ignition control, fire protection and emergency procedures.
Regulatory Activity operated under incorrect approvals. Confirm precise activity classification before operation.
Risk should be managed before it becomes a cost. 17
Implementation

12-MONTH ZERO-TO-SCALE ROADMAP

Month 0–1 — Market mapping

Build supplier and buyer lists, classify material grades, obtain indicative offers and understand transport routes. No machinery.

Month 1–2 — First brokerage transactions

Execute small, buyer-confirmed transactions and document weight, quality, purchase price, selling price and total cost.

Month 2–4 — Repeat profitable routes

Identify repeat suppliers, repeat buyers and the grades producing the best risk-adjusted contribution.

Month 3–6 — Selective aggregation

Introduce short-term storage or micro sorting only where commercial evidence proves additional value.

Month 6–12 — Build finance-ready history

Create formal monthly reporting, turnover history, supplier/buyer concentration analysis and working-capital records.

After evidence — Processing expansion

Evaluate baling, cutting, shredding and fiber opening only using measured transaction and processing economics.

Scale only after the previous stage has been commercially proven. 18

Purchase price/kg

02

Selling price/kg

Actual buyer offer or transaction price by grade.

03

Handling / conversion cost/kg

Transport, sorting, labor, packaging, losses and processing.

04

Available tons/month

Repeatable supply rather than one-time offers.

05

Saleable yield

Usable output divided by purchased input.

06
